If you are planning a small trip — a long weekend, a city break with a difference — Tivoli Gardens in Copenhagen is hard to beat as a centerpiece. The park opened on 15 August 1843, making it the second-oldest operating amusement park in the world, and with 4.25 million visitors in 2024 it is the most visited amusement park in Scandinavia. It sits right next to Copenhagen Central Station, which makes it a perfect anchor for a short stay: you can be inside one of Europe's most charming historical playgrounds within minutes of stepping off the train.

<!-- more -->

### A Pleasure Garden, Not Just a Theme Park

Tivoli owes its existence to a clever pitch. Founder Georg Carstensen obtained a charter from King Christian VIII by arguing that "when the people are amusing themselves, they do not think about politics," and was granted roughly 15 acres of the city's fortified glacis. The result was never a plain amusement park: even today it feels closer to a Victorian pleasure garden, with historic gardens, concert halls, a theatre and rides woven together. The name was originally "Tivoli & Vauxhall," after the Jardin de Tivoli in Paris and Vauxhall Gardens in London.

The park's influence reaches far beyond Denmark. Walt Disney visited in 1951 and is said to have taken inspiration from it for Disneyland — stroll through Tivoli's flower beds and lamp-lit paths and you can see why. Locals grew up here, which keeps it from ever feeling purely touristy.

### What You'll Find Inside

Tivoli packs a surprising amount into its city-centre grounds: 25 rides, four roller coasters and two water rides. The star is the Rutschebanen, a wooden roller coaster from 1914 and one of the oldest operating wooden coasters in the world — still running, still thrilling, with a brakeman on board. Around it you'll find modern thrill rides, the historic Pantomime Theatre, the Tivoli Concert Hall, and in summer the free Friday Rock concerts.

The real magic, though, is the atmosphere. Come at dusk once during your trip: the gardens light up with thousands of lamps, and the whole park turns into a glowing wonderland that has charmed visitors for nearly two centuries.

### When to Go and What It Costs

Tivoli runs in seasons rather than year-round: a summer season from late April to September (open until 11 pm), a Halloween season in October, and a much-loved Christmas season in November and December with lights, markets and mulled wine. It closes between seasons, so check the dates before planning around it.

Pricing is à la carte: entry starts from around 150 DKK and varies by date (quieter days are cheaper), while rides need either individual tickets or an unlimited-rides wristband. A combined entrance-and-rides package is usually the best value, and Tivoli is included in the Copenhagen Card. Most visitors find three to five hours about right — and a sit-down dinner inside the gardens is a genuinely pleasant way to spend an evening.

### How a Small Trip Would Look

A Tivoli-centred city break writes itself. Arrive, drop your bags at the hotel (the neighbourhood around the Central Station is full of options), and spend your first evening in Tivoli — the lights are at their best after dark. Use the next day for the classic Copenhagen loop: Nyhavn, the Little Mermaid, the Strøget shopping street and a canal boat tour, then return to Tivoli for a Friday Rock concert or a ride on the wooden coaster as the sun goes down. If you have a third day, add the National Museum or a stroll through Christiania before heading home.

Getting there is easy from the Netherlands. It's roughly an hour and a half by plane from Amsterdam, while the train takes about 11 to 13 hours with a change in Hamburg. And from spring 2028 there will be an even nicer option: European Sleeper is launching a direct night train from Amsterdam to Copenhagen and Malmö — leaving around 7 pm and arriving the next morning, with tickets starting at roughly €60–80. It's the return of a route that disappeared when the Danish night train was cancelled in 2014, and for a short city break it turns the journey itself into part of the holiday. Whether you come by air, rail or overnight sleeper, a small trip to Tivoli feels like stepping into a storybook — and then back out again, straight into the middle of Copenhagen.
